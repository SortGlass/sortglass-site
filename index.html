Browse

Start with what you want to organize.

Eighteen thousand photos feels like a wall. Browse narrows it to something you can actually finish: pick a focused set, then open it in whichever view suits the job.

  • All or UnsortedStart with everything, or only the items that aren’t in any album yet.
  • Jump to a YearYear tiles count the matching items from each year. Tap one to start there in Organize.
  • Focus with ProCombine filters like Favorites, File Type, Albums, and Rated, then save the combinations you use often.
  • Two Ways InOpen the results in Library for a grid, or in Organize to review them one at a time.

Why start here?

Decisions come easier when the set is small and coherent. One year, or everything that isn’t in an album yet, is a session you can finish, and finishing sessions is what keeps a library under control.

Organize

A little focus. A lot of progress.

One photo fills the screen. Every decision is a single gesture, and the next photo is already waiting. Try it on the phone. Drag the photo.

  • Swipe to Keep MovingSideways to move between photos, down to Favorite, up to send to Trash.
  • Give Photos a HomeTap an album beneath the photo to add it. A filled icon means it’s already there, so a second tap removes it. A photo can live in several albums.
  • Pick Your Strongest ShotsWith Pro on iOS 27 or later, tap a star to rate. Ratings are saved to Apple Photos itself.
  • Undo, Redo, and HistoryThe curved arrows reverse recent actions. History keeps your last 100 Album and Trash actions.

Why one at a time?

Culling is a string of small judgments. Showing a single photo, large, removes the noise of a grid, and giving each judgment its own gesture means you never reach for a menu. Trash is only a queue, so moving fast never costs you a photo you meant to keep.

Library

The bigger picture, all in one place.

When you want to see patterns rather than single frames, Library shows everything, or just the set you filtered in Browse.

  • Choose Your GridPinch between 3 and 8 photos per row, or pick a density in Grid Style. It remembers your choice.
  • Work with Several at OnceHold and sweep to select, then send them to Trash, an album, or Favorites in one step.
  • Revisit Your RatingsShow stars on the grid to compare your picks side by side (iOS 27 or later).
  • Find Your PlaceDrag the date scrubber to jump through months and years.

Why a second view?

Organize is for deciding; Library is for checking. Seeing your five-star picks next to each other, or a whole album at a glance, shows you what reviewing one photo at a time hides.

Albums

Everything in its place.

Albums is where your structure lives. Search it, open any album to browse, and decide which ones earn a spot in Organize.

  • Browse and ManageSearch albums and folders, open one to browse, or choose Organize Album to review it one photo at a time.
  • Keep Favorites ClosePin the albums you use most. They appear right beneath the photo in Organize.
  • Arrange Your WorkflowReorder pinned albums so the ones you reach for first come first.
  • Folders, TooCreate albums and folders, and move albums into folders.

Why pin?

The album row in Organize only has room for what you really use. Pinning keeps your handful of true destinations one tap away, while the rest of your albums stay tidy here instead of crowding every decision.

Design Principles

Decide once.
Move on.

Every part of SortGlass is built around keeping you moving without ever putting your library at risk.

Built on Apple Photos

No importing and no second library. Favorites, Albums, Ratings, and deletions happen in the Apple Photos library you already use, and sync with iCloud Photos.

Feels Like Part of iOS

Gorgeous Liquid Glass on iOS 26 and later, with native materials on iOS 18. Designed with accessibility in mind, with support for Reduce Motion, Dynamic Type, and VoiceOver.

Nothing Deleted by Accident

Swiping up only adds an item to SortGlass Trash. You review the queue and confirm with iOS before anything moves to Recently Deleted, where it stays recoverable for 30 days.

Undo Anything

Undo and Redo sit beside every photo. History keeps your last 100 Album and Trash actions, so you can step back even after you’ve moved on.

One Gesture per Decision

Swipe up for Trash, swipe down for Favorite, and tap an Album to file a photo. Swipe distances are tuned so deliberate moves count and accidental brushes don’t.

Privacy in Mind

SortGlass works on your device: no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and your photos are never uploaded to a server.

Questions

Good to know.

Does SortGlass delete my photos?

Only when you confirm. Swiping up adds a photo to SortGlass Trash, which is just a queue. When you choose Delete in Trash, iOS asks you to confirm, and confirmed items move to Recently Deleted in Apple Photos, where Apple generally keeps them for up to 30 days.

Does it work with iCloud Photos?

Yes. SortGlass works directly on your Apple Photos library, so favorites, albums, ratings, and deletions sync through iCloud Photos according to your settings. Photos stored only in iCloud may download when you open them.

What do I need to use it?

An iPhone running iOS 18 or later. Star ratings require iOS 27 or later, and the Liquid Glass design appears on iOS 26 and later.

Is SortGlass free?

It’s free to download, and the free version includes up to 100 Trash and Album actions each week. SortGlass Pro unlocks unlimited organizing, ratings, advanced and saved filters, and every theme and icon.

How do I cancel or restore a purchase?

Manage or cancel subscriptions in Settings → your name → Subscriptions. To restore Pro on a new device, open More → SortGlass Pro → Restore Purchases while signed in with the same Apple Account.

What does SortGlass collect about me?

Nothing. SortGlass has no accounts, ads, or analytics, and it never uploads your photos. Optional iCloud backups are off by default and stay in your private iCloud. See the Privacy Policy for details.

Is there an iPad or Mac version?

Not yet. SortGlass is designed for iPhone today. iPad and Mac versions may come in the future, for seamless integration across your devices and even better workflows on larger screens.
